Environment:
Early 1990s Yugoslavia, primarily set in Belgrade. The city is filled with tension—stadiums echo with chants turning into battles, streets are marked by protests and unrest, and a nation once unified by sport is now splitting apart. War is on the horizon, and football is no longer just a game.

Premise:
A young Red Star Belgrade player stands at a crossroads as his country collapses. Should he fight for Serbia, leave for an international career, or stay to keep Red Star’s legacy alive? Pressure mounts from all sides—friends, family, club officials, nationalists, and ultras—all demanding different choices. His decision will not only define his career but could also determine life and death.

Exposition:
Red Star Belgrade has just won the 1991 European Cup, reaching the pinnacle of football. But as war brews, nationalist divisions take over the stadiums, turning rivalries into ethnic battles. The protagonist, a talented Serbian midfielder, is caught in the storm—his teammates from different republics are forced to pick sides, while Red Star’s ultras become radicalized. He must decide his future while navigating friendships, politics, and personal survival.

First Act:
The protagonist experiences growing tensions in the dressing room between Serbian and non-Serbian players.
Nationalist chants and fights at games escalate, and ultras become more aggressive.
A meeting with Red Star officials, where he is told foreign clubs want to sign him, but staying means becoming a club icon.
Friends start receiving military draft papers.
A violent incident at a match against Dinamo Zagreb brings the situation to a breaking point.
Point of View:
Third-person (following the protagonist’s internal struggles and external challenges).
